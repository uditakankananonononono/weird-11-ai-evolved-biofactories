\section{Introduction}
Engineered microbes convert renewable feedstocks into fuels, materials, and chemicals, but the design of production strains remains guided primarily by human intuition: an engineer picks a pathway, expresses it, and hopes the host tolerates it. An alternative is to let a search process explore the space of possible metabolic architectures and select for what works, under conditions that resemble an industrial process rather than a single idealized optimum. This project asks whether a computational evolutionary search, starting from a target product rather than an existing pathway, can discover non-obvious biological architectures that match or exceed known engineered pathways under multi-condition robustness criteria. The design principles that let engineered cells maintain high productivity while remaining metabolically stable are the scientific object of interest; the evolved architecture library is the deliverable.

Three hypotheses are pre-registered (PRE-REGISTRATION.md, locked before any data or search): H1 (performance) - at least one evolved architecture reaches predicted product flux at or above the published engineered pathway evaluated in the same genome-scale model under identical uptake bounds; H2 (novelty) - the evolved library contains architectures absent from the published pathway that are mechanistically interpretable; H3 (robustness principle) - architectures selected under multi-condition pressure retain their production across a locked stress battery at a significantly higher rate than architectures selected under single-condition pressure. Honest negatives are preserved and redirect the project internally; no negative is published as the final result.

\section{Pre-registered design}
\subsection{Hosts, models, and targets}
The primary host is \emph{Escherichia coli} K-12 MG1655 in the genome-scale metabolic model iML1515, with the yeast arm (Yeast8) reserved for cross-host robustness analysis. Three locked target products span distinct chemistry classes: 1,4-butanediol (non-natural industrial diol; benchmark route Yim et al.\ 2011, the Genomatica pathway), isobutanol (branched-chain higher alcohol; benchmark Atsumi et al.\ 2008, the keto-acid route), and lycopene (tetraterpene; benchmark Alper et al.\ 2005, crtEBI with dxs push). Reaction lists per the cited papers were locked in data/benchmarks.json before any search, with DOI provenance.

\subsection{Benchmark metric: production envelopes}
For each target, the published engineered pathway was implemented in iML1515 under the locked base condition (M9 minimal, glucose 10 mmol/gDW/h, aerobic). Because production competes with biomass, a single max-growth number is meaningless: at 100\% of maximum growth all three pathways carry zero product flux. The benchmark is therefore a production envelope: maximum product flux with growth fixed at locked fractions of the FBA maximum,
\begin{equation}
F_t(g) = \max_{v} \; v_{\text{product}} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad S v = 0, \; v_{\text{biomass}} = g \cdot \mu_{\max}, \; l \le v \le u,
\end{equation}
evaluated at $g \in \{1.0, 0.9, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 0.0\}$ with a parsimonious-FBA tie-break. Measured envelopes (results/benchmark\_flux.json): 1,4-BDO reaches $F=10.35$ mmol/gDW/h at $g=0$ and $5.27$ at $g=0.5$; isobutanol $10.00$ and $5.24$; lycopene $0.97$ and $0.49$. The envelope, not a single titer, is what an evolved architecture must beat at matched growth.
Table~\ref{tab:envelopes} reports the measured envelopes for all three published routes at the six locked growth fractions; the FBA maximum growth rate of the host under the base condition is $\mu_{\max} = 0.877$ h$^{-1}$ for all targets (identical uptake bounds, wild-type core).

\subsection{Search protocol}
The tie-break inside each envelope point is parsimonious FBA: among optimal solutions, minimize total flux,
\begin{equation}
v^{*} \;=\; \arg\min_{v} \; \|v\|_{1} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad S v = 0, \; v_{\text{product}} = F_t(g), \; v_{\text{biomass}} = g\,\mu_{\max}, \; l \le v \le u,
\end{equation}
with the pFBA optimality slack fixed at 0.999; FBA and pFBA product fluxes were verified equal on all envelope points (unique optima), so the cheaper FBA is used for search fitness and full pFBA reserved for final evaluation.
The search genome encodes on/off choices over a locked reaction pool (benchmark route blocks plus literature-noted alternatives, data/reaction\_pool.json). Fitness is product flux at the pFBA-optimal solution with growth fixed at $g=0.5$, the envelope midpoint. Search fitness is the nominal-condition envelope point at the locked growth fraction,
\begin{equation}
\Phi(a) \;=\; F_a^{(\mathrm{nominal})}(0.5),
\end{equation}
and architecture performance against the benchmark route is reported as the exceedance ratio
\begin{equation}
E_a \;=\; \frac{F_a^{(\mathrm{nominal})}(0.5)}{F_{\mathrm{benchmark}}^{(\mathrm{nominal})}(0.5)},
\end{equation}
with $E_a > 1$ required for an H1 beat at matched uptake bounds. The exhaustive pools used so far have genome spaces of
\begin{equation}
|\mathcal{G}| \;=\; 2^{h} \times 2^{t},
\end{equation}
genomes per target ($h$ host-level blocks, $t$ target-specific blocks: $h{=}0, t{=}2$ in pool v1 giving 4; $h{=}4, t{=}2$ in pool v2 giving 64), so enumeration evaluates every genome exactly once and the stochastic search budget is dominated by construction. Significance of any evolved advantage is calibrated against a 1000-seed random-architecture null as the percentile
\begin{equation}
\hat{q} \;=\; \frac{1 + \sum_{s=1}^{1000} \mathbf{1}[\Phi(a_s^{\mathrm{rand}}) \ge \Phi(a)]}{1001}.
\end{equation}
The search is a ($\mu+\lambda$) evolutionary algorithm ($\mu=24$, $\lambda=48$, tournament selection, 60 generations, five locked seeds), with a 1000-seed random-architecture null for significance calibration. On the version-1 pool (two alternative blocks per target, four genomes), exhaustive enumeration strictly dominates the stochastic search and was used instead; the EA scaffold is retained for expanded pools.

\subsection{Stability battery}
A scalar summary of production-growth coupling used in the envelope analysis is the local slope
\begin{equation}
\sigma_t(g) \;=\; \frac{F_t(g_2) - F_t(g_1)}{g_2 - g_1},
\end{equation}
the shadow price of growth in units of product flux per unit growth fraction; the measured envelopes are strictly monotone with $\sigma_t < 0$ throughout.
Robustness is evaluated over a locked nine-condition battery (nutrient shifts, carbon shifts to glycerol and acetate, anaerobiosis, uptake perturbations, oxygen limitation). After a metric-interpretation bug was caught at first evaluation (growth referenced to the base-condition maximum, which made every condition untestable), the locked rule is growth fixed at 50\% of the condition-specific maximum, with retention defined against nominal-condition flux. The nine locked conditions are: GLC\_LOW (glucose uptake $-2$), GLC\_MID ($-5$), GLC$+20\%$ ($-12$), GLC$-20\%$ ($-8$), GLYCEROL (sole carbon, uptake $-10$), ACETATE (sole carbon, uptake $-10$), ANAEROBIC (oxygen uptake 0), O2$-50\%$ (oxygen uptake halved), and the nominal base GLC\_AEROBIC (glucose $-10$, aerobic); all uptakes in mmol/gDW/h. Formally, for architecture $a$ and condition $c$,
\begin{equation}
R(a, c) \;=\; \frac{F_a^{(c)}(0.5)}{F_a^{(\mathrm{nominal})}(0.5)}, \qquad
\mathrm{BatteryPass}(a) \;=\; \bigwedge_{c \in \mathrm{battery}} \mathbf{1}\!\left[ R(a,c) \ge 0.8 \right],
\end{equation}
where $F_a^{(c)}(0.5)$ is the architecture's product flux in condition $c$ with growth fixed at $50\%$ of the condition-specific maximum. The pass bar of $0.8$ was locked before any architecture was evaluated against the battery. What this battery measures is a model property - retention of modeled product-flux capability under perturbation - not, by itself, evidence of growth-coupled production robustness; the growth floor's status is examined per pool in the caveats.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Single-condition search reproduces the benchmark envelopes}
Across all fifteen seed runs (three targets, five seeds), the best evolved architecture exactly reaches the locked envelope value at $g=0.5$: 5.27 mmol/gDW/h for 1,4-BDO, 5.24 for isobutanol, 0.49 for lycopene. The search recovers the published routes as optimal under single-condition selection - a validation of the evaluation pipeline, and the baseline for H1.

\subsection{Search definitions (locked terminology)}
To remove any ambiguity about what the method is: the \emph{representation} is a genome vector of discrete, biologically typed block choices ($h$ host-level levers and $t$ target-specific route blocks over the locked reaction library); the \emph{search policy} is either exhaustive enumeration of the genome space (strictly dominant at pool sizes $\le 256$ and used for all reported results) or a $(\mu{+}\lambda)$ evolutionary algorithm ($\mu=24$, $\lambda=48$, tournament selection, 60 generations, five locked seeds) retained for larger pools; the \emph{objective} combines nominal-condition product flux against the matched-growth published-route envelope and the tiered robustness score $R_{\mathrm{tiered}}$ over the locked nine-condition battery; the \emph{learning component} is none - no trained, fitted, or adaptive model appears anywhere in the pipeline, and no parameter is updated from data. The method is constraint-based evolutionary search over a genome-scale stoichiometric model, nothing more. Where a future amendment adds a learned surrogate or policy, it will be locked and named explicitly.

\subsection{H2 candidate: a tail-only minimal 1,4-BDO architecture}
A non-obvious architecture emerged from the search: with \emph{both} heterologous upstream route blocks switched off, the four-step heterologous tail (4-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase, CoA transferase, CoA-acylating aldehyde dehydrogenase, alcohol dehydrogenase) still reaches the full nominal flux of 5.27 mmol/gDW/h. The model explains why: \emph{E. coli}'s native GABA shunt (succinate-semialdehyde supply through glutamate metabolism) feeds the tail without any heterologous upstream enzyme. This tail-only architecture is absent from the Yim et al.\ pathway definition, uses four fewer heterologous enzymes, and is mechanistically interpretable - the H2 novelty pattern in its locked form at the nominal condition. As the tiered-battery subsection below shows, the candidate does not survive the battery, so the claim stands at nominal condition only.

\subsection{H3 honest negative on pool v1, and a battery-design finding}
Exhaustive multi-condition evaluation of pool v1 found that \emph{no} genome passes the locked battery (80\% retention in all nine conditions): best minimum retentions are 0.175 (1,4-BDO), 0.176 (isobutanol), and 0.27 (lycopene). Inspection shows the failure is structural, not a lack of search power: under the severe nutrient shift GLC\_LOW (glucose at 2 rather than 10 mmol/gDW/h), elementary carbon balance caps a C4 product at roughly one to two mmol/gDW/h against a nominal 5.27, a retention ceiling of 0.2--0.3 that no architecture can lift. The bound is elementary: a C4 product made from glucose at uptake $u_{\mathrm{glc}}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{product}} \;\le\; \frac{6}{4}\, u_{\mathrm{glc}} \;=\; 3.0 \quad \text{mmol/gDW/h at } u_{\mathrm{glc}} = 2,
\end{equation}
before any carbon is allocated to biomass, maintenance, or byproducts; with growth fixed at $50\%$ of the condition maximum the achievable product flux falls to roughly one to two, giving $R(a, \mathrm{GLC\_LOW}) \le 1.5/5.267 \approx 0.28$ for every conceivable architecture $a$ - including the published routes and any architecture the search could ever return. The benchmark engineered routes fail identically. A pass bar of 80\% retention on severe nutrient shifts is therefore unsatisfiable by construction, the battery provides no selection gradient, and H3 is untestable as locked. This is recorded as an honest negative with the design flaw named, and a redesign (retention against condition-achievable optimum rather than against nominal flux; tiered bars for shifts versus perturbations; robustness-oriented pool levers such as knockout blocks and co-utilization feeds) is being locked as a dated amendment before any re-evaluation, following the project's redirect-on-failure rule.

\subsection{Pool v2: robustness levers shift the nominal optimum (amendment 2026-09-26)}
Following the redirect rule, pool v2 was locked as a dated amendment before evaluation: four host-level robustness levers - acetate-overflow knockout (ackA--pta), formate-overflow knockout (pflB), lactate-overflow knockout (ldhA), and a glycerol co-utilization feed - added to the target-specific blocks, expanding the genome space to 64 genomes per target. Exhaustive enumeration (192 genomes total, metric-independent of the pending battery redesign) was again strictly dominant over the stochastic budget at this size. Under the nominal condition at $g=0.5$, the co-utilization feed dominates every target: best nominal fluxes rise to 6.496 (1,4-BDO), 6.498 (isobutanol), and 0.730 (lycopene) mmol/gDW/h, against envelope values of 5.267, 5.239, and 0.488 - nominal-condition exceedances of 23\%, 24\%, and 50\% over the published routes evaluated in the same model. The gain is attributable to the added carbon supply, so the honest comparison under co-feeding must re-baseline the benchmark route with the same feed block enabled:
\begin{equation}
E_a^{\mathrm{cofeed}} \;=\; \frac{F_a^{(\mathrm{cofeed})}(0.5)}{F_{\mathrm{benchmark}}^{(\mathrm{cofeed})}(0.5)},
\end{equation}
with both sides evaluated under identical uptake bounds including the glycerol co-feed. That re-baselined comparison has now been executed (amendment 2026-09-27 10:04): $E^{\mathrm{cofeed}} = 1.00$ on all three targets (6.496 vs 6.496; 6.498 vs 6.498; 0.730 vs 0.730) - the nominal exceedance is entirely attributable to the added carbon supply, and no nominal-flux advantage over the published routes survives equal-feed comparison. The 1000-draw random-architecture null likewise recovers the pool optimum on all three targets, confirming that nominal-condition search is trivial at this pool size; the project's discriminating content is the robustness structure below, not nominal flux. An honest secondary negative is recorded: for isobutanol, some knockout combinations abolish product flux entirely (minimum 0.0), so the levers are not uniformly benign even at nominal condition.

\subsection{Tiered battery executed: satisfiability restored, H3 advantage unearned (amendment 2026-09-27)}
The battery was redesigned and locked as a dated amendment on 2026-09-27 before evaluation: mild perturbations (glucose $\pm 20\%$, oxygen $-50\%$) are scored as absolute retention versus nominal with a hard leg of $\ge 0.8$ each, and severe nutrient shifts (GLC\_LOW, GLC\_MID, ANAEROBIC, GLYCEROL, ACETATE) are scored against the pool frontier in the same condition, $r_k = v_{p,k} / \max_a v_{p,k}(a)$, with a hard leg of $\ge 0.8$ each; $R_{\mathrm{tiered}} = 0.5\,R_{\mathrm{pert}} + 0.5\,R_{\mathrm{shift}}$. The bar is now satisfiable: 16/64 (1,4-BDO), 18/64 (isobutanol), and 18/64 (lycopene) genomes pass both hard legs. The best designs score $R_{\mathrm{tiered}} = 1.0$ in all three targets: pflB knockout plus glycerol co-feed plus the succinyl-CoA route at 6.496 mmol/gDW/h nominal (1,4-BDO), pflB knockout plus co-feed plus the NADH alcohol dehydrogenase at 6.497 (isobutanol), and co-feed plus idi push at 0.730 (lycopene). Two findings follow. First, the tail-only H2 candidate does \emph{not} survive the tiered battery ($R_{\mathrm{tiered}} = 0.8023$ under the corrected-semantics re-evaluation below, superseding the GLPK-era $0.768$ by explicit diff; severe-condition frontier-relative retention 0.41--0.67 on GLC\_LOW, GLC\_MID, ANAEROBIC, and ACETATE, confirmed unchanged): its minimal-architecture claim stands at the nominal condition only, and the battery-robust designs are co-fed, not enzyme-minimal. Second, the H3 comparison at this pool size is corrected from the GLPK-era record: the superseded battery recorded the best-by-nominal and best-by-$R_{\mathrm{tiered}}$ genomes as coinciding in all three targets (3/3), reported at the time as an honest negative. The deterministic re-evaluation below finds coincidence in one of three targets (lycopene only): at 1,4-BDO and isobutanol the nominal-best genome ties the battery-best at the nominal condition ($6.4956$ and $6.4975$ mmol/gDW/h, identical at full printed precision) but is not battery-optimal ($R_{\mathrm{tiered}} = 0.9492$ and $0.9923$ versus $1.0$). At pool 64 a nominal-only search therefore misses the robust optimum in two of three targets at tied nominal fitness - tie-level evidence in the direction of the robust-selection principle, explicitly superseding the earlier honest-negative framing; the effect is a tie-break, not a flux gap. The queued resolution was executed under the 2026-09-27 10:04 amendment: pool v3 (two further host levers, adhE and tpiA knockouts; 256 genomes per target, exhaustive) again yields $\mathrm{argmax}(v_{\mathrm{nominal}}) = \mathrm{argmax}(R_{\mathrm{tiered}})$ in all three targets (rank-1 difference 0.0000 everywhere), so the H3 search-mode advantage is not earned at N=256 either - a second falsification, reported as such. Battery pass rates at N=256: 32/256 (1,4-BDO), 60/256 (isobutanol), 22/256 (lycopene). The same amendment's H2 redirect (Robust Minimal Architecture: minimum heterologous enzyme count $K_{\mathrm{het}}$ subject to battery pass, published anchor $K_{\mathrm{het,\,Yim}} = 5$) is also falsified on pool v3: no battery-passing 1,4-BDO design with $K_{\mathrm{het}} \le 4$ exists - every robust design requires an upstream route block, so the published route's enzyme count is not beatable under the robustness constraint in this pool. The remaining live test of the robustness principle is the held-out-target arm (3-HP), locked in the same amendment.

\subsection{Objective ablation (amendment 2026-09-27 12:37 IST, locked before computation)}
Scoring-component ablation on the committed exhaustive pool-v3 results (the EA itself is inapplicable to ablate at this pool size - the space is enumerated 256/256 - so the operative units are the objective's components). Four re-rankings were compared per target: full $R_{\mathrm{tiered}}$, nominal-flux only (robustness removed), $R_{\mathrm{pert}}$ only, and $R_{\mathrm{shift}}$ only. On all three targets all four arms select the \emph{same} winning design; the severe-retention loss of the nominal-only ranking is exactly 0.0. Recorded verbatim per the locked pass rule: the robustness objective is non-binding at pool v3 - robustness and nominal flux are co-maximized by the same architectures on this pool. This is disclosed as a pool property; on larger pools where the trade-off binds, the objective can matter, and the claim made here is only the measured one. Diversity and constraint-penalty ablations are deferred with disclosure (EA-search components, not defined for exhaustive evaluation).

\subsection{Unrestricted versus biologically constrained search (amendment 2026-09-27 12:38 IST, locked before computation)}
Dropping the growth constraint and maximizing product flux directly over the same pool yields, per target, a measured statement of what the constraint buys. On 14BDO the unrestricted winner reaches 7.75 mmol/gDW/h against the constrained best's 6.50 (ratio 0.838) and is growth-viable, but it fails the committed battery outright (worst severe-condition retention 0.46): the unconstrained optimum overpromises at nominal and collapses under condition shifts, which is the realism function of the constrained objective in one number. On isobutanol the unrestricted winner is itself battery-robust (0.91), so the constraint is non-binding there; on lycopene the two optima agree within solver tolerance (ratio 1.011, recorded rather than smoothed). No unrestricted top-ten design is zero-growth on any target. The constraints therefore cost nominal flux only where they also buy measured robustness - a target-dependent answer, reported verbatim.

\subsection{What the search is, precisely (queue item 16; definitional pass)}
To remove any vagueness about the computational method: the \emph{representation} is a binary genome over six host-lever blocks and two target-route blocks (256 architectures per target); the \emph{search policy} at pool v3 is exhaustive enumeration of that space - the earlier $(\mu+\lambda)$ evolution strategy was retired once enumeration became strictly dominant, so no stochastic search enters any reported number; the \emph{objective} is the locked tiered robustness score $R_{\mathrm{tiered}} = 0.5 R_{\mathrm{pert}} + 0.5 R_{\mathrm{shift}}$ evaluated by constraint-based flux analysis at 50\% condition-specific growth; and the \emph{learning component} is nil - nothing in this pipeline learns from data. It is exact enumeration with a pre-registered scoring rule, and the paper's claims are limited to what that procedure supports.

\subsection{Prioritized experimental shortlist (queue item 17; candidates only, no experiments)}
The results yield a ranked, falsifiable validation program. (1) \emph{14BDO constrained-versus-unrestricted A/B}: implement both the $R_{\mathrm{tiered}}$ winner (\texttt{ko\_pflB} + \texttt{cofeed\_glycerol} + route B) and the unrestricted winner (cofeed only, no route block); the framework predicts the unrestricted design retains $< 0.5$ of nominal production under GLC\_LOW/ANAEROBIC while the constrained design retains $\ge 0.8$ - a two-strain experiment that can kill the approach. (2) \emph{Isobutanol winner confirmation}: the same architecture class is battery-robust in silico (worst severe 0.91); a single-condition-shifts panel on one constructed strain tests the tiered battery's severe-leg calibration directly. (3) \emph{Lycopene non-binding check}: constrained and unrestricted optima coincide within tolerance, so lycopene is the negative-control arm - the prediction is no measurable robustness difference. Ordering is by expected information per construct: (1) tests the framework's central claim, (2) calibrates its battery, (3) controls for over-crediting the constraint.


\subsection{Pool v4: a five-arm evolutionary operator race at $N=16{,}384$ (amendments 4a71551, eede3fd, 2026-09-28)}
The pool-v4 expansion adds six further host levers (ppc, gnd, pgi, me1, acs, icdhyr knockouts) to pool v3's six, giving twelve host levers plus two target-specific route levers: $2^{14} = 16{,}384$ genomes per target. (An early amendment draft mislabeled this as $2^{16}=65{,}536$ by double-counting pool v3's two target levers; the error was caught and corrected in PRE-REGISTRATION.md before any result depended on it, and the executed design is the intended one.) At this size enumeration no longer dominates the stochastic budget by the locked margin, so the pool is the first on which the evolutionary algorithm itself is the instrument - and the first on which search \emph{operators} can be compared meaningfully. The locked experiment is an operator race: five arms share the $(\mu{+}\lambda)$ configuration ($\mu=24$, $\lambda=48$, 60 generations, tournament $k=3$, bitflip $p=1/n$, 2{,}904 evaluations per run, 17.7\% of the space) and differ in one operator each - A0 baseline; A1 constraint-aware mutation (re-sample until unseen); A2 diversity tie-break in tournament selection; A3 adaptive mutation rate on stagnation; A4 surrogate-assisted selection (random forest, 50 trees, default hyperparameters, trained on the run's own evaluation history). The primary cohort is 75 runs (5 locked seeds $\times$ 5 arms $\times$ 3 targets) with a Mann--Whitney gate (arm versus A0 on best flux, exact two-sided, $p<0.05$) locked outcome-blind, a 10{,}000-label permutation sensitivity pooling a labeled 8-run extension cohort that is never mixed into the primary gate, and the final tiered battery on every run's best genome with the severe frontier computed within the evaluated set (disclosed: 25 primary best-genomes per target, not the full pool). Two engineering records belong to the methods rather than the results: the implementation checkpoints every generation so that a killed run resumes bit-exactly (verified by interrupted-versus-uninterrupted equivalence), and a GLPK presolve variant was tested as a solver-speed candidate and rejected after 1646/2568 re-evaluated fitnesses mismatched the committed values (maximum relative difference 13.45) - the solver configuration is part of the locked protocol, not a tuning knob.

\textbf{The nominal optimum is flat.} All 75 primary runs - every arm, every seed - converge to the same best flux per target: 13.4545 (1,4-BDO), 12.3333 (isobutanol), and 1.3704 (lycopene) mmol/gDW/h, roughly double the pool-v3 optima, so the six new host levers raise the nominal ceiling substantially, but no arm finds more of it than any other. The locked gate fires three times: isobutanol A1 ($p=0.0079$), lycopene A2 ($p=0.0079$), lycopene A3 ($p=0.0079$), with the permutation sensitivity in agreement ($p=0.0002$, $0.0078$, $0.0077$); all remaining cells sit at $p=0.31$--$1.0$. These passes are reported verbatim per the lock - and immediately qualified: the differences they detect are at the tenth to thirteenth significant digit of the flux (relative effect size $\sim 10^{-11}$), which is GLPK tie-breaking noise among equally optimal genomes, not an operator effect. Statistically decidable, scientifically zero. No arm produces a materially higher nominal flux than the baseline on any target, and at a flat optimum nominal fitness cannot in principle rank operators.

\subsection{Solver artifact in the tiered battery: detection, repair, and explicit supersession (amendments 2026-09-28 16:18--16:46 IST, each locked before its computation)}
While instrumenting the A5 follow-up experiment below, the pool-v4 battery evaluation was found to depend on GLPK warm-start state rather than only on the LP being solved. The proof is direct: the search fitness and the battery's nominal GLC\_AEROBIC leg solve the \emph{identical} LP (same base bounds, same $0.5\,\mu_{\max}$ floor), yet the committed battery recorded nominal $0.0$ for four 1,4-BDO best genomes whose recorded search fitness is $13.4545$; live, the same genome evaluated $13.4545$ on a cold solver, $0.0$ after a solver reset, and drifted after roughly one hundred warm evaluations. An interim repair (maximum of three perturbed GLPK solves per LP, locked 16:22 IST) reduced but did not eliminate the failures - 762 of roughly 2{,}000 genomes still failed self-validation - and was itself superseded with disclosure. The final repaired metric (locked 16:46 IST) exports each LP to matrices and solves it with HiGHS via \texttt{scipy.optimize.linprog}, which carries no state between solves; the metric is deterministic. \section{Caveats}
(1) All results are in-silico flux predictions; no wet-lab validation is claimed, and no build-phase engineering guidance is produced. (2) Both pools evaluated so far are deliberately small; the exhaustive results bound what these pools can express, and conclusions about search power await larger pools. In particular, the H3 multi-condition advantage was first recorded as undetectable at pool 64 under the superseded GLPK-era battery; the deterministic re-evaluation finds the nominal-best and battery-best designs coincide in only one of three targets there, at tied nominal fitness, so pool-64 evidence is tie-level and pool-256 remains the falsifying scale. (3) The tail-only H2 candidate is verified at the nominal condition only: it fails the tiered battery, every battery-passing design requires the glycerol co-feed, and under equal-feed comparison ($E^{\mathrm{cofeed}} = 1.00$) the co-feed fully accounts for the nominal-flux gains - no flux advantage over the published routes remains. The defensible claims are the robustness structure and its cross-target motif, not nominal flux. (4b) H3's multi-condition search advantage is falsified at N=256 (nominal-best and battery-best designs coincide in every target, rank-1 difference 0.0000); at N=64 the corrected-semantics re-evaluation reverses the superseded GLPK-era coincidence reading (coincidence in one of three targets, at tied nominal), so the earlier N=64 honest-negative framing is withdrawn. (5) At pool v4 the nominal optimum is flat and fully reached by every operator arm tested; operator claims are therefore limited to tie-breaking effects on battery robustness, and the three locked-gate passes are numerically significant solver-noise effects ($\sim 10^{-11}$ relative), reported verbatim and not counted as arm advantages. (6) At pool v4 the growth-coupling floor is non-binding: maximum growth under the locked base condition evaluates to numerical zero ($\sim 5\times 10^{-31}$) for measured genomes, so the $0.5\,\mu_{\max}$ floor never constrains and all pool-v4 fluxes are effectively uncoupled maxima; mild-leg retentions above 1 (perturbed conditions yielding more product than nominal) are deterministic properties of this model's bound set, reported verbatim and not claimed as biology. (7) Battery solver history: the pool-v4 battery was first computed under GLPK with carried warm-start state (artifact, superseded), then under a max-of-3 perturbed GLPK repair (insufficient - 762 self-validation failures, superseded), and finally under the deterministic HiGHS metric with 100\% nominal-versus-search validation across $\sim$1{,}706 genomes; only the HiGHS numbers are current, and the superseded values remain in the record for audit. (8) The pool-v2/v3 batteries were computed under the same GLPK pipeline; they have since been re-evaluated under deterministic HiGHS with the original metric semantics (amendment 70a6872, locked before computation), after a first re-evaluation execution contained an adaptation defect - a missing biomass unpin that artifactually zeroed three severe legs - caught by a pre-fold-in hand check, withdrawn, and retained in the record. Under the corrected re-evaluation every published pool-v2/v3 number re-stands except the two tie-level pool-64 coincidence flags and the tail-only $R_{\mathrm{tiered}}$ ($0.768 \to 0.8023$); all diffs appear explicitly in the text. (4) GSMM predictions assume steady state and fixed biomass composition; real titers are typically lower.

\subsection{Per-architecture rationale: why the battery winners win}
Each battery-winning genome is interpretable block by block, and the interpretation is the same logic the tiered battery was designed to select for. \textbf{1,4-BDO winner} (\texttt{ko\_pflB} + glycerol co-feed + succinyl-CoA route, nominal 6.496 mmol/gDW/h, $R_{\mathrm{tiered}}=0.994$): the pflB knockout removes the formate overflow arm, keeping pyruvate carbon inside the central network where the succinyl-CoA route can draw on it; the co-feed adds a second, more reduced carbon source whose uptake is uncoupled from glucose repression in the model. The design trades nothing at nominal and retains frontier-relative flux on every severe shift except anaerobiosis (0.938), where formate removal is nearly free because pyruvate formate-lyase is itself oxygen-sensitive. \textbf{Isobutanol winner} (\texttt{ko\_pflB} + \texttt{ko\_adhE} + co-feed + NADH-alcohol dehydrogenase, 6.498, $R_{\mathrm{tiered}}=0.9997$): the adhE knockout blocks the ethanol drain on acetyl-CoA and NADH, and choosing the NADH-dependent alcohol dehydrogenase matches the cofactor pool the knockoffs enrich - the architecture is internally redox-consistent, which is exactly the property the mild oxygen-limitation leg (retention 1.0) measures. \textbf{Lycopene winner} (co-feed + idi push, 0.730, $R_{\mathrm{tiered}}=1.0$, the only perfect score): the idi push balances IPP:DMAPP isomer supply - the known bottleneck of the heterologous crtEBI tail - while the co-feed raises precursor flux without touching dxs; the design reaches the battery frontier in all nine conditions, including anaerobiosis, where its retention is 1.0. Common logic across all three: carbon supply is the binding lever - \emph{every} battery-passing genome in all three targets carries the glycerol co-feed (32/32, 60/60, and 22/22 of passers), while no genome passes without it, and conservation knockouts are optional score-improving refinements rather than requirements (co-feed-only genomes pass in all three targets; the rank-1 designs pair the co-feed with a knockout in two of three). The design principle the battery surfaces at N=256 is therefore sharper than a generic conjunction: unlock carbon supply first, then conserve it. This is a statement about which levers robust designs share, not about a multi-condition search advantage - H3 was falsified at N=256 (argmax coincidence, above) and nothing here resurrects it.

\section{Solver and metric provenance}
All flux evaluations solve linear programs over the iML1515 genome-scale model. Search fitness and every superseded battery used GLPK 5.0 through optlang 1.9.1; all current battery numbers use HiGHS through \texttt{scipy.optimize.linprog} (scipy 1.15.3, \texttt{method='highs'}, default tolerances) on exported arrays. The export procedure is fixed: after applying the genome and the condition's exchange bounds, the stoichiometric matrix is taken from cobra 0.32.1 (\texttt{create\_stoichiometric\_matrix}, lil converted to csc), the equality right-hand side is zero (steady state), and column bounds are read directly from the reaction objects. A solver reset means a fresh, independent \texttt{linprog} call per LP; no basis, objective, or model state is carried between any two solves, and genome application is rolled back per genome by context manager. The per-leg evaluation algorithm is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item unpin the biomass reaction (bounds $0$ to $1000$) and solve for the true condition-specific maximum growth $g$;
\item if the LP fails or $g \le 0$, the leg value is $0.0$;
\item otherwise fix biomass at $(0.5\,g, 0.5\,g)$ and solve for maximum product flux $p$; the leg value is $p$ ($0.0$ on failure).
\end{enumerate}
The locked validation algorithm adjudicates every battery value before use: for every evaluated genome the battery's nominal-condition value must agree with that genome's recorded search fitness within $10^{-9}$ relative, and every mismatch is disclosed verbatim. Fresh-process reproduction with independently written code was performed for both the cohort battery and the pool re-evaluation (54/54 legs each, tolerance $10^{-9}$). Superseded values never enter current statistics, figures, or denominators; they appear only as explicitly labeled historical audit data. The evolutionary search configuration is locked at MU $=24$, LAM $=48$, 60 generations, tournament selection with $k=3$, bit-flip mutation at rate $1/n$, and $(\mu+\lambda)$ truncation replacement, on seeds 260927--264927.
